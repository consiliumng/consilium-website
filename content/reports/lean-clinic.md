A ₦100 million health centre is a building. Whether it works depends on what it costs to run, and who pays. This report asks that question first of clinic operators, who must decide whether electronic records, rapid tests and point-of-care analysers will pay for themselves, and then of the planners, insurers and partners who fund primary care.

In our illustrative model, a lean clinic earns about ₦2.8 million a year more than a paper one at 20 patients a day, and pays back in about 21 months. Below about 14 patients a day it earns no more. The family saves time and money on every visit, and running costs overtake the build cost in about 2.3 years. The model is built on stated assumptions, the weakest of which a 90-day pilot could replace with measurements.
