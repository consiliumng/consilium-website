## LinkedIn (main post, attach `lean-clinic-post.png` or the 7-slide carousel)

Everyone is asking what ₦100 million builds. A primary health centre. The better question is what keeps it running.

Official estimates for a full centre sit close to ₦100m to ₦120m. In our model, running a lean clinic costs about ₦43m a year before rent, so running costs pass the build cost in about 2.3 years.

We have published a break-even guide for clinic operators and primary care planners. What it finds:

- Households pay 72% of Nigeria's health spending out of pocket. Per person, Nigeria spends more than Rwanda, which has far better outcomes. How money is organised matters as well as how much there is.
- A child with fever can take 5¾ hours to treat in a traditional clinic and about 75 minutes in a lean one, and the family pays about ₦4,000 less (illustrative).
- For the operator, the case is real but modest. At 20 patients a day the lean set-up earns about ₦2.8m a year more and pays back in about 21 months. Below about 14 a day it earns no more than a paper clinic.
- The biggest lever, more patients from faster service, is also the one no Nigerian study has measured. We suggest a 90-day pilot.

It is an illustrative model on stated assumptions, not a forecast. Every input is listed in the appendix.

Report (PDF) and chart previews: https://consilium.ng/reports/lean-clinic/

## Newsletter (Substack) blurb

Subject: What keeps a ₦100 million health centre running?

The public conversation is about what it costs to build a primary health centre. Our new report asks what it costs to run one, starting from the clinic operator's side: when do electronic records, rapid tests and point-of-care analysers pay for themselves, and what do they change for the family? The answer is honest and conditional. Break-even is about 14 patients a day, payback about 21 months at 20 a day, and the biggest benefit (more patients from faster service) is the one nobody has measured yet. Read the report, check the assumptions, and put your own numbers in. https://consilium.ng/reports/lean-clinic/

## Direct note to partners (NHIA, state ministries, development partners)

We have published a break-even guide to lean diagnostics in Nigerian clinics, with specific asks of insurers and funders: pay for point-of-care tests in the tariff, fund consumables, power and data as well as buildings, and back a 90-day pilot that measures time, family cost and tests per visit. If you want to discuss the pilot design, write to office@consilium.ng.
